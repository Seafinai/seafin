How it works

  1. New receipts and invoices are collected from the portal and the mailbox. Account numbers are hidden.
  2. AI reads each one: date, vendor, amount and tax.
  3. AI suggests the account, based on how you recorded that vendor before.
  4. The totals are checked and a draft is saved in QuickBooks.
  5. A bookkeeper approves each draft. Nothing counts until then.

6 Not worth doing yet

  • AI answering clients’ tax questions. A wrong answer costs more than the time saved.
  • Month-end checks. QuickBooks bank rules already do most of it, and the rest needs judgement.
  • Tax organiser intake. Worth about 70 hours a season, but it depends on your tax software. Revisit after the first build.
  • Engagement letter renewals. About 12 hours a year, and a Word mail merge handles it without AI.

7 Keeping client data safe

Where it runs

We recommend Claude through a business account that doesn’t train on your data. If your insurer or your engagement letters limit where client data can go, it runs inside your firm’s own Amazon cloud account instead. Either way, you can switch it off at any time.

What gets hidden

  • Bank and card account numbers are masked down to the last 4 digits before anything leaves your systems.
  • Routing numbers and Social Security numbers are removed completely.
  • Mostly handwritten documents skip AI and go straight to a bookkeeper.
  • Working copies are deleted after 30 days. Your originals stay where they are today.

Questions to ask before the build

  1. Do your engagement letters allow outside services to process client documents? Do clients need to be told?
  2. Do your letters or your insurer limit where client data can be stored, for example to the United States?
  3. Does your insurance policy require you to report a new service that handles client data?

Tax preparers have extra federal rules on client information, so check with your adviser before the trial.
