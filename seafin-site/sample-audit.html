Summary

Three tasks take 146 staff hours a month: typing client documents into QuickBooks, chasing clients for missing paperwork, and writing up client calls. AI can take about 95 of those hours. At $45 an hour for bookkeeping time, that is roughly $4,300 a month. Start with document intake. It is the largest saving, the best-proven, and a bookkeeper still approves every entry.

Top three opportunities

1. Client documents into QuickBooks as drafts

Receipts and invoices that clients email or upload are read, matched to the right vendor and category, and created as draft entries in QuickBooks Online. A bookkeeper reviews and approves each one instead of typing it.

Not recommended now

  • AI answering clients’ tax questions. The risk of a wrong answer outweighs the time saved. Keep a person on every reply.
  • Automating reconciliations. QuickBooks bank rules already handle most of it; the remaining cases need judgement.

Where it should run

Client documents contain account numbers and tax IDs. Two setups work:

  • Claude, through a business account whose terms exclude your data from model training, with account numbers masked before anything is sent. Faster to set up, cheaper, and more accurate.
  • An open-source model on a small server in your office or a private cloud. Nothing leaves your control; more setup and somewhat lower accuracy.

Recommendation: start with Claude, unless your engagement letters or your insurer forbid sending client data to a third party. Check both before the build.

90-day plan

  1. Days 1 to 14

    Fixed quote for opportunity 1. Confirm data rules with your insurer and engagement letters. Pick five pilot clients.

  2. Days 15 to 45

    Build document intake. Run it on the five pilot clients alongside the current process and measure hours per client.

  3. Days 46 to 75

    If pilot intake is under 1 hour per client, roll out to all 40 clients and add the missing-items emails.

  4. Days 76 to 90

    Review the measured savings. Decide on call notes, or stop there.

What to measure: intake hours per client per month; the share of drafts approved without edits; days from month end to closed books.
