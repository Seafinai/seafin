Three tasks take 146 staff hours a month: typing client documents into QuickBooks, chasing clients for missing paperwork, and writing up client calls. AI can take about 95 of those hours. What we found

Your team spends 206 hours a month on four recurring tasks, and another 120 hours each tax season on organiser intake. The largest block is typing: 100 hours a month spent reading receipts and invoices, keying them into QuickBooks Online and picking the account. Your bookkeepers already know how to do every step of that work, and reading and sorting documents is what current AI does well. Reconciliations take 60 hours, but QuickBooks bank rules already handle most of them, so little is left to save there.

What we recommend

Build document intake first. AI reads each receipt and invoice and creates a draft entry in QuickBooks Online, and a bookkeeper approves it. That saves about 65 hours a month, worth $2,900. If the pilot works, add the month-end missing-items email (20 hours, $900). For call notes, first try the meeting recap in Microsoft 365 Copilot, Microsoft’s AI assistant, if you already pay for it. Build only if it falls short (10 hours, $450).

Run it on Claude, Anthropic’s AI model, through a business account whose terms exclude your data from training. Account numbers are masked (blacked out) before anything is sent. If your engagement letters or your insurer limit where client data can go, run it in your own Amazon Web Services (AWS) account instead.

Cost and payback

The build for document intake is a fixed price. The illustrative figure for this firm is $8,000. AI usage costs about $40 to $100 a month for roughly 2,400 documents. The intake saving alone repays the build in under 3 months. If the saving turns out to be half our estimate, payback takes about 6 months.

The decision we ask for

Approve a fixed quote for document intake and a pilot on five clients. In the first two weeks, you confirm your data rules with your insurer and your engagement letters. At day 45 we compare measured intake time per client with today’s 2.5 hours. Under 1 hour, we roll out to all 40 clients. Over 1 hour, we adjust or stop before you spend more.

How it works

Each step uses the cheapest tool that does it well. A model is the AI system that reads and writes text. A small, fast model handles routine reading. A frontier model, the most capable and most expensive kind, makes the judgement calls. Plain code, meaning ordinary software rules with no AI, does anything with a certain answer.

  1. Plain code

    Collect. Every hour, new files from the portal and the intake mailbox are picked up and filed against the client.

  2. Plain code

    Mask. Account numbers, routing numbers and tax IDs are found on each page and blacked out before anything leaves your systems. Mostly handwritten documents go straight to a bookkeeper.

  3. Small · Haiku 4.5

    Read. Claude Haiku 4.5 returns the date, vendor, amounts, tax and line items. About 1 in 10 documents, such as a blurry photo, gets a second read from Claude Sonnet 5.5, a mid-size model that sits between the two in cost.

  4. Frontier · Opus 5.5

    Choose the account. Claude Opus 5.5 sees the client’s chart of accounts and how this vendor was coded before, and returns an account with a one-line reason.

  5. Plain code

    Check. Totals add up, tax matches the rate, the date falls in an open period, the entry is not a duplicate, and the matching bank-feed transaction is found. Anything that fails is flagged for the bookkeeper.

  6. Plain code

    Draft. The entry is created in QuickBooks Online as a draft, with the document attached.

  7. A person

    Approve. The bookkeeper reviews the draft beside the document, edits it if needed, and approves it. Nothing reaches the books before this step.

Data it touches

Receipts, invoices and statements your clients send; each client’s chart of accounts and vendor list; past entries for the same vendor. It does not touch bank logins, payroll or tax returns.

Approval and controls

  • Nothing posts to the books until a bookkeeper approves it.
  • Each draft shows the suggested account, the reason and the source document side by side.
  • Each week a partner checks 20 approved entries picked at random.
  • Every draft, edit and approval is logged with the name of the person who made it.
  • Corrections are saved as vendor rules, so the same fix is not needed twice.
  • A monthly spending cap stops AI usage above $150.

7 Not recommended now

  • AI answering clients’ tax questions. The risk of a wrong answer outweighs the time saved. Keep a person on every reply.
  • Automating reconciliations. QuickBooks bank rules already handle most of it; the remaining cases need judgement.
  • Tax organiser intake and prior-year comparison, for now. Together they could save about 110 hours a season, but both depend on the tax software, which we did not look at. Revisit them after the first build.

8 Where it should run

Client documents contain account numbers and tax IDs. Three setups work:

  • Claude direct, through a business account whose terms exclude your data from model training, with account numbers masked before anything is sent. Fastest to set up and cheapest.
  • Claude inside your own AWS account, through Amazon Bedrock (AWS’s service for running AI models), in a US region (AWS data centres in the United States), under AWS’s compliance terms. AWS doesn’t share the data with Anthropic. A little more setup; the same accuracy.
  • An open-source model on a small server in your office. An open-source model is one anyone can download and run on their own hardware. Nothing leaves the building; the most setup and somewhat lower accuracy.

Recommendation: start with Claude direct. If your engagement letters or your insurer limit where client data can go, use your own AWS account instead. Check both before the build.

Questions for your engagement letters and insurer

  1. Do your engagement letters allow you to use outside service providers to process client documents, and do they require you to tell clients?
  2. Do they, or your insurer, limit where client data may be stored or processed, for example to the United States?
  3. Does your cyber or professional liability policy require you to tell the insurer about a new service that handles client data, or require specific controls?
  4. Does your written information security plan need to list the new service? The FTC Safeguards Rule expects tax preparers to keep one.

If the answer to the second question is yes, or clients must agree before an AI service sees their documents, use the AWS route in section 8. Tax return information also has its own federal disclosure rules for preparers (Internal Revenue Code section 7216). Some of your bookkeeping clients are also tax clients, so ask your adviser how those rules apply before the pilot.

Assumptions

  • $45 an hour for bookkeeping time.
  • 40 monthly clients at about 60 documents each: about 2,400 documents a month.
  • About 300 tax returns a year. Organiser intake takes 120 hours between February and April.
  • Intake today: 2.5 hours (150 minutes) per client per month, from two timed client months.
  • Intake after: 52.5 minutes per client. That is 60 approvals at 30 seconds, 12 edits at 1.5 minutes and 3 hand-typed documents at 1.5 minutes.
  • Missing-items emails: 45 minutes per client today and 15 after; about 3 emails per client a month.
  • Call notes: 48 calls a month, 20 minutes each today and 7.5 after.
  • Running cost: the token counts in section 11, at published prices for October 2026, with no batch discount.
  • The $8,000 build cost is illustrative. A real quote follows the audit.
  • Savings are counted in staff time. Whether that time goes to more clients or fewer overtime hours is your choice; we assume neither.

Glossary

API
The connection a system offers so other software can read from it or write to it, such as creating a draft entry in QuickBooks.
Model
The AI system that reads and writes text. Claude is Anthropic’s family of models.
Small, fast model
A cheaper model for routine steps such as reading a receipt. Here, Claude Haiku 4.5.
Mid-size model
Between small and frontier in cost and ability. Here, Claude Sonnet 5.5.
Frontier model
The most capable and most expensive kind of model, used for judgement calls. Here, Claude Opus 5.5.
Plain code
Ordinary software rules with no AI, used where the answer is certain, such as checking that totals add up.
Token
The unit AI usage is priced in: a piece of a word. 1,000 tokens is roughly 750 words.
Cached input
Instructions and reference data that repeat from one request to the next, billed at about a tenth of the normal price.
Batch processing
Sending work to run within a day instead of at once, at half price.
Masking
Blacking out sensitive numbers before a document is sent anywhere.
Draft entry
An entry in QuickBooks Online that waits for a bookkeeper’s approval before it counts in the books.
Bank rules
QuickBooks settings that categorise recurring bank transactions automatically.
Transcript
A written record of what was said on a call.
AWS
Amazon Web Services, Amazon’s cloud computing service.
Amazon Bedrock
AWS’s service for running AI models, including Claude, inside your own AWS account.
US region
AWS data centres in the United States, chosen so the data is processed there.
Open-source model
A model anyone can download and run on their own hardware.
Two-step sign-in
A login that needs a second check, such as a code on a phone, as well as the password.
Pilot
A trial on a few clients, run alongside the current process, to measure the real saving before rolling out.
Payback
The months it takes for the monthly saving to repay the build cost.
