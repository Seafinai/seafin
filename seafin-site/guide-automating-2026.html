What small businesses are automating in 2026

Eight real examples from the past year, from a cheese maker to a painting contractor. These are other companies’ results, not ours, taken from the press and from the AI companies’ own write-ups, which we label. Checked 5 October 2026.

Finding money in the paperwork

Rebel Cheese, Austin

An AI checks every weekly shipping invoice line by line against the company’s carrier contract, and checks photos of boxes for size errors. It flags each overcharge with the proof; the CEO files the disputes.

Result, as reported: about $250,000 in overcharges found after one holiday season, and about $400,000 saved in a year. Fortune, independent.1

Answering the phone and booking the work

Bill Joplin’s Air Conditioning & Heating, Dallas-Fort Worth

A phone agent answers, looks up the customer’s history and the technicians’ schedules, and books the job. Calls it can’t handle go to staff.

Result, as reported: over 1,300 calls in about three months, more than 90% booked, 72% handled start to finish, and about $74,000 in booked revenue. ServiceTitan’s own release, so vendor-published.3

Echo Janitorial, 16 cleaners

An AI receptionist takes up to 15 calls an hour and sends new-client details straight into the company’s scheduling software. The owner also uses AI to sort email and project cash flow from bank statements.

Result, as reported: the receptionist costs $99 a month against about $4,000 for a person, and the owner’s working day fell from 19 hours to 8. Revenue also grew, though not only because of AI. Fortune, independent.2

Valley Diabetes & Endocrinology, Weslaco, Texas

An AI system helps with patient check-in and makes follow-up calls after visits about blood sugar and supplies. Nurses stay in charge.

Result, as reported: a nurse task that took about 40 minutes per patient now takes under 5, according to the vendor. Local TV, relaying the vendor’s claim.5

Replacing software that held them back

A five-person trucking-compliance firm, Tennessee

After its software vendor gave 30 days’ notice, the firm rebuilt its fuel-tax filing system with Claude.

Result, as reported: errors fell from 7% to zero, and the same staff now handle twice their old busiest month. Anthropic’s own write-up, so vendor-published.4

Liberty Trailers, Indiana

The owner built a reconciliation tool for a newly bought factory, and the IT director replaced paper production schedules with live dashboards.

Result, as reported: the tool took about 15 minutes; the dashboards took days rather than months. Anthropic’s own write-up.4

Turning drawings and paper into numbers

A painting contractor, Chicago area

Claude reads PDF blueprints floor by floor and works out the numbers for a bid. The owner checks the work and asks it to show its reasoning.

Result, as reported: no figures given; the owner reports catching its mistakes by checking the measurements himself. Anthropic’s own write-up.4

A commercial real-estate lender, about 30 staff, Northern California

Loan officers’ incoming email is compared with the current rate sheet and a reply is drafted; the firm’s lawyer uses AI for a first pass on leases. Staff review every draft.

Result, as reported: no figures given. CIO, independent.6

What they have in common

  • Each started with one job that took hours every week.
  • The AI does several steps in the company’s own systems, such as reading, checking and booking.
  • A person still approves the last step, such as the dispute, the booking or the filing.
  • Most are small teams, from two people to a few dozen.
