Off-the-shelf AI tools or a custom build?

Most small businesses should try the ready-made AI features first. They are cheaper, quicker to start and often enough. A custom build pays off for a narrower set of jobs. Here is how to tell which you have.

What ready-made tools do now

The main AI products now work inside software many small businesses already use.

  • Claude for Small Business, launched by Anthropic on 13 May 2026, connects Claude to QuickBooks, PayPal, HubSpot, Canva, Docusign, Google Workspace and Microsoft 365. It comes with ready-made workflows for jobs such as invoicing, payroll planning and marketing.1
  • ChatGPT has apps and connectors that link it to common business tools. Check whether yours are on the list.
  • Microsoft 365 Copilot works inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and Teams. It is an add-on to a qualifying Microsoft 365 business plan.2

Each is a monthly fee per person, with nothing to build. Someone still has to set it up well: decide who gets access, connect the right accounts and write instructions for your common jobs.

When ready-made is enough

  • A person starts each job and checks the result: drafting a reply, summarising a contract, tidying a spreadsheet.
  • Your main systems are on the supported list.
  • The work changes from day to day, so a fixed process wouldn’t help much.
  • The data is ordinary business information, not patient records or similar.

In our sample audit, one of the three jobs, notes after client calls, was set aside for the Microsoft 365 Copilot feature the firm may already pay for. The advice was to build only if that fell short.

When a custom build pays off

  • Systems with no ready connection. Industry software, older accounting systems, and supplier or carrier portals you can only reach through a website login.
  • Work that must run unattended. Sorting the inbox overnight or checking every invoice as it arrives, with a person approving only what matters.
  • Customer-facing agents. A phone agent that answers and books jobs, or a chat on your website, needs your rules, your calendar and a clean handover to staff.
  • Compliance. Patient records under HIPAA need a setup covered by the right agreements, often inside your own cloud account. See our HIPAA guide.
  • Volume. When the same job happens hundreds of times a month, a few minutes saved on each adds up.

Before you switch one on

  • Decide who gets access, and to which accounts. Start with the people who will use it most.
  • Check that it asks a person before anything is sent, posted or paid, and leave that setting on.
  • Read where your data goes and on what terms. Our guide on what happens to your data covers the main providers.
  • Keep the instructions you write in a shared place, so they stay with the business when staff change.

Try ready-made first

  1. Pick one job that eats hours every week.
  2. Try it for two to four weeks with the tool you already have, or the closest ready-made option. Note where it falls short.
  3. If the gaps are the ones above, such as a system it can’t reach or work that has to run on its own, that is where a build earns its cost.

The two also work side by side: Claude or ChatGPT for everyday work, and one custom build for the job those tools can’t reach. Our pricing page shows what each costs.
